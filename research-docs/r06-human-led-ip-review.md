# r06 — Human-Led IP Review for Agent-Assisted Commerce

## TL;DR

The IP, trademark, copyright, and marketplace-risk gate for Neon Ronin should be **human-led**.

Agents may discover demand, propose ideas, gather context, surface possible red flags, and organize evidence. They should **not** make final IP determinations, assign final legal risk levels, or decide which ideas move forward.

Core principle:

> **Agents discover and propose. The human decides.**

This applies especially to product ideas, niche choices, marketplace suitability, design direction, listing text, IP/common-sense risk, and anything that could go live on Etsy, Printify, Fiverr, Gumroad, Creative Market, or similar platforms.

---

## 1. Operating Principle

Neon Ronin should treat human review as the main control layer for idea selection and IP-sensitive decisions.

The system is not intended to be a fully autonomous product publisher. It is intended to be an agent-assisted research and drafting system where agents help surface opportunities and prepare review materials, while the human operator remains the decision-maker.

### Core rule

```text
Agents propose.
Human decides.
Nothing goes live without approval.
```

### Practical meaning

Agents can:

- find demand signals
- identify keyword opportunities
- summarize competitor patterns
- propose product/service ideas
- propose personalization hooks
- draft product concepts
- draft design prompts
- draft listing copy
- gather supporting context
- surface possible red flags
- suggest safer alternatives
- queue ideas for review

Agents should not:

- decide which ideas become products
- decide which niches to enter without approval
- make final IP/trademark/copyright determinations
- assign final risk levels
- publish listings
- activate marketplace items
- delete listings
- message customers
- issue refunds
- change marketplace/account settings

---

## 2. Updated Idea Workflow

The idea workflow should be:

```text
Demand signal found
→ Agent proposes idea
→ Agent explains supporting evidence
→ Human reviews the idea
→ Human approves / edits / rejects / parks
→ Only approved ideas move forward
```

This keeps agents in the scout/analyst role and keeps the human operator in the strategist/editor role.

The agent can rank or group ideas for convenience, but a score should never equal approval.

---

## 3. Human-Led IP Review Flow

The IP-sensitive flow should be:

```text
Agent detects demand
→ Agent generates idea
→ Human reviews IP/trademark/copyright risk
→ Human reviews supporting evidence
→ Human approves / edits / rejects
```

Important distinction:

Agents may gather context and surface possible red flags, but they should **not** run the final IP check, make the final legal call, or assign the final risk level.

The human reviewer owns that decision.

---

## 4. Main Human Gates

There should be four major human gates.

| Gate | Human decision |
|---|---|
| **Idea gate** | Is this product/service idea even worth pursuing? |
| **IP/common-sense gate** | Does this feel risky, derivative, brand-adjacent, celebrity-adjacent, franchise-adjacent, or otherwise dangerous? |
| **Design/listing gate** | Is the design, product concept, and listing good enough? |
| **Publish gate** | Should this go live or trigger a marketplace-changing action? |

These gates are separate on purpose. An idea can pass the demand gate and fail the IP/common-sense gate. A design can pass the IP gate and fail the quality gate. A listing can pass quality review and still be held back at publish review.

---

## 5. What Agents Should Surface for Review

Agents should prepare a review packet for the human rather than making the decision themselves.

Useful review packet fields:

- proposed product/service idea
- source keyword or demand signal
- source platform, such as Etsy, Google, Pinterest, TikTok, Amazon, Reddit, DataForSEO, or another provider
- short explanation of why the idea might be worth considering
- competitor examples or observed marketplace patterns
- suggested product type
- suggested buyer/persona
- suggested personalization hooks
- proposed title/tag/copy angles
- proposed design prompt or creative direction
- possible brand/franchise/celebrity/entity red flags
- possible quote/lyrics/slogan red flags
- any obvious marketplace policy concerns
- safer alternative angles
- notes on whether the idea should be parked, revised, or rejected

The agent should be treated like a research assistant building the packet, not the judge.

---

## 6. Human Review Questions

The human reviewer should be able to answer these questions before moving an idea forward.

### Idea quality

- Is this something buyers would actually want?
- Does the idea fit the current business direction?
- Is it specific enough to have buyer intent?
- Is there a personalization angle?
- Is there enough margin or commercial potential?
- Is this differentiated enough from obvious marketplace slop?

### IP / trademark / copyright / common-sense risk

- Does the idea reference a protected brand, franchise, sports team, celebrity, university, movie, show, book, video game, anime, musician, song, lyric, or viral phrase?
- Does the design direction resemble a protected character, brand style, or logo?
- Is the phrase likely to be trademarked in the relevant product class?
- Is this “inspired by” language risky?
- Is the idea based on a trend that is actually protected IP?
- Would this make a reasonable marketplace reviewer nervous?

### Marketplace fit

- Would this fit Etsy’s seller-designed / original-design expectations?
- Would this require AI disclosure?
- Would this require production partner disclosure?
- Does the listing risk looking like spam, duplicate content, or low-effort AI output?
- Does this need legal review before continuing?

Some ideas should be easy rejects before deeper work.

Examples:

```text
Taylor Swift retirement candle
```

The system can surface these as obvious concerns, but the human should still own the final decision to reject or revise.

## 8. Records to Keep

Because the project relies on human review and seller involvement, records matter.

The system should preserve:

- original demand signal
- agent idea proposal
- supporting evidence shown to the human
- human decision: approve, edit, reject, or park
- human notes or edits
- prompt history for generated designs
- listing draft versions
- disclosure notes
- final approval timestamp
- final publish approval timestamp, if applicable

This supports quality control, repeatability, and evidence of seller involvement.

---

## 9. Research Implications

Future research should assume:

1. The human is the final gate for idea selection.
2. The human is the final gate for IP/common-sense review.
3. Agents can gather and organize evidence but should not make final legal determinations.
4. Agents can suggest safer alternatives but should not automatically transform risky ideas into products.
5. No marketplace-changing action should happen without explicit approval.

This means the product is not an “autonomous store operator.”

It is better described as:

> **A human-led command center where agents scout demand, draft options, and prepare evidence for review.**

```text
Always-on research is allowed.
Autonomous publishing is not.
Agents scout.
Agents draft.
Agents organize.
```

Short version:
